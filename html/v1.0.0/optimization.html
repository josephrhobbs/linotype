Optimization

is an extraordinarily general paradigm for design and inference under uncertainty. Given some function \( f(x) \) and some constraints \( g(x) = 0 \) and \( h(x) \le 0 \), what is the value of \( x \) that minimizes \( f \) and satisfies all constraints? This is the central question in optimization, and rich bodies of literature have evolved over recent decades, approximately classified by the structure of each of the functions \( f \), \( g \), and \( h \).

Here, I summarize several algorithms for multivariable optimization. I have made the intentional choice to focus Linotype's summary and application on a specific subfield of optimization referred to as convex optimization, so named because it focuses on the optimization of convex functions over convex feasible sets.

Convex optimization algorithms have two particularly nice properties. Firstly, they always return global optimizers (and never "get stuck" in local, non-global optimizers). Secondly, they require no special "tricks" for initialization.

Readers of Linotype will notice that many other topics covered here draw on convex optimization. In a way, it is the lingua franca of Linotype. I have chosen to make convex optimization the lingua franca of "modern" schools of control, estimation, and design. A wealth of critical results in these fields can be demonstrated using a single convex optimization program. We aim to embrace convexity wherever we find it, and to encourage it when at first we perceive a problem to be nonconvex.
